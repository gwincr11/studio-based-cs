SOFTWARE ENGINEERING

A CONCRETE ADR EXAMPLE

Architecture Decision Records

ADR 001 / SHIFT SIGNUPSILLUSTRATIVE PROPOSAL
Context
Two people may try to claim the last available shift.
Options
Validate only in the interface, or enforce capacity in the backend and datastore.
Decision
Use an atomic backend operation to enforce capacity; use the interface for feedback.
Tradeoff
More backend complexity in exchange for data integrity under concurrent use.
Evidence
Test concurrent claims: capacity is respected and the unsuccessful user is told why.
Use this as a concrete continuation of introducing ADRs. Students should explain context, options, relevant quality attributes, the decision, and consequences. Ask them to show that the implemented system honors the decision. “The agent recommended it” is not sufficient reasoning.

Usability, reliability, availability, performance, scalability, security, privacy, and modifiability.

TRADEOFFS

No system maximizes everything.

Make qualities concrete through scenarios: a member claims a shift on a phone, or many members sign up at once. Add measurable criteria with the stakeholder rather than inventing thresholds. Attribute-driven design connects those needs to structures and materials.

Example prompts, not assigned solutions. Adapt to the other team's problem. Method: LUMA Institute: Creative Matrix.

Introduce LUMA Institute's Creative Matrix as a structured way to generate varied ideas quickly. LUMA recommends columns related to people or problems and rows that enable solutions, such as technologies, environments, or policies. This adapted example uses moments in the other team's workflow as columns and people, tools, and process changes as enablers. Spend one minute replacing the headings with categories relevant to the problem you just heard. Spend three minutes writing or sketching ideas at different intersections, one idea per sticky note; both teammates contribute and defer evaluation. Aim for variety rather than filling every cell. Spend the last minute comparing ideas and choosing one promising direction and an assumption to test in the next slide's prototype. The five-minute timing and example prompts are suggestions for this workshop, not a prescribed LUMA format. Source: https://www.luma-institute.com/creative-matrix/
